# Holoscopic Boundary Computing

**Polarized Near-Field Diffraction and Coherent Interference Observation**

**Author**: Chur Chin (Busan, Republic of Korea)  
**Date**: May 13, 2026  
**Version**: v1.0 (Preliminary)

## Project Overview

A low-cost experimental platform using a **smartphone, linear polarizing film, and a nail clipper** (as a precise rotation stage) to observe **near-field Fresnel diffraction**, polarization-dependent interference, and coherent optical effects.

The setup demonstrates that complex coherent optical phenomena can be systematically studied with extremely simple and inexpensive tools.

---

## Experimental Setup

- **Light Source**: Red laser pointer (highly coherent, ~650 nm)
- **Polarizer**: 3×3 cm linear polarizing film fixed with black tape on a nail clipper
- **Rotation Mechanism**: Nail clipper’s natural pivot axis — provides stable and precise angular control
- **Observation Method**: Near-field projection onto white paper or wall (patterns visible only at very close distance, < 5–8 mm; disappear beyond ~1 cm)
- **Configurations Tested**: Both **input** and **output** positions

---

## Key Observations

### 1. Linear Fringes (Multiple Parallel Lines)

- Strong **multiple bright/dark linear fringes** appear in the extreme near-field.
- Fringe number, contrast, and direction change dramatically with **rotation angle** of the nail clipper.
- **Output position**: Maximum intensity near **+45°**
- **Input position**: Maximum intensity near **-10°** (optimal angles are opposite due to mounting direction)
- Classic signature of **Near-field (Fresnel) diffraction** at straight edges (film edge, tape, clipper structure).

### 2. Dynamic Interference inside Circular Spot

At ~0° in output position, small movements cause visible **interference pattern changes** inside the main laser spot. (See Video 2: `media/holoscopic-boundary/videos/circular_interference.mp4`.)

### 3. Double Spot Phenomenon

When an additional loose polarizing film is moved in front of the output assembly, **two distinct circular spots** appear and disappear periodically. (See Video 1: `media/holoscopic-boundary/videos/20260513_194532.mp4`.)

### 4. Satellite Pattern

Observed once previously (moiré-like satellite concentration), but not reproduced in this session.

---

## Physical Interpretation

The observed effects are combinations of well-known classical optical phenomena:

- **Near-field Fresnel diffraction** at straight edges
- **Polarization modulation** (Malus’ law affecting contrast and visibility)
- **Multiple reflections / thin-film interference** (responsible for double spots)
- **Coherent source** enabling high-contrast interference

This setup serves as an excellent, controllable platform for studying **polarization-dependent near-field coherent optics**.
